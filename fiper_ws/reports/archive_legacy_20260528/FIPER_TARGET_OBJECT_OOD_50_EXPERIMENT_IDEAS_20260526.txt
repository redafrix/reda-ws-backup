FIPER TARGET-OBJECT OOD 50-EXPERIMENT DESIGN
Date: 2026-05-26

Goal
----
Improve the three target-object OOD metrics at the same time:
1. high OOD failure detection,
2. low OOD success false alarms,
3. early warning on OOD failures.

Current failure mode
--------------------
The current action-only RND + ACE OR q95 monitor detects fold_01 OOD failures,
but it alarms on nearly every successful OOD target-object rollout. The issue is
not that the dataset is invalid. The issue is that the monitor still behaves too
much like a distribution-shift detector instead of a conditional risk detector.

Baseline evidence from fold_01:
- Held-out picked objects: butter and chocolate_pudding.
- OOD failure episodes: 35.
- OOD success episodes: 183.
- OR q95 K=3 detects all OOD failures early, but also false-alarms on 100% of
  OOD successes.
- ACE is more robust than action RND but later.
- RND is early but overfires under target-object shift.

Main campaign idea
------------------
Use fold_01 as a development fold. Train many candidate monitors with high max
epochs and early stopping. Never train, calibrate, or early-stop on held-out OOD
target-object rows. Rank candidates by OOD failure early detection minus OOD
success false alarm burden.

All thresholds must be calibrated from success_calib_seen only.

Model families
--------------
Family A: success-only anomaly models
- RND over action chunks.
- RND over action summaries.
- Conditional RND using action + proprio + object-distance set.
- RND with 4/8/16-step history.
- Autoencoder and denoising autoencoder variants.

These preserve the success-only spirit of FIPER, but may still overfire on OOD
successes if the learned normality manifold is too narrow.

Family B: supervised seen-failure risk models
- These train on success_train_seen as negative and failure_eval_seen as
  positive.
- OOD target-object success/failure rows stay evaluation-only.
- These are not pure success-only FIPER, but they test whether seen-object
  failure examples can teach a risk boundary that generalizes to unseen picked
  objects.

Family C: temporal models inspired by old archaeology
- TCN_history_k8 is the most important old idea to retry.
- GRU/LSTM/Transformer variants test whether temporal history reduces false
  alarms by distinguishing "new but controlled" from "new and failing".
- Inputs use previous proprio/executed_action/reward/success history plus
  current candidate action chunk and optional ACE/context.

Family D: ablations
- No object-position set.
- No current proprio.
- ACE-only.
- Short history vs medium history vs longer history.
- BCE vs focal loss vs positive weighting.

Why these are not cheating
--------------------------
- OOD target objects are never used in train, early stopping, or calibration.
- The supervised jobs may use seen-object failures, but this is a different
  model family and must be reported as supervised risk, not success-only RND.
- Thresholds still come from success_calib_seen.
- Task ID, suite ID, target-object label, and language are not used as model
  input.
- Object positions are represented as anonymous distance-sorted numeric sets,
  not object-name slots.

What counts as improvement
--------------------------
A candidate is interesting if it improves over current OR q95 K=3 on fold_01 by:
- reducing success_test_ood episode false alarm substantially below 100%,
- keeping failure_eval_ood detection high,
- keeping Det@25 useful,
- not exploding success_test_seen false alarms.

Suggested decision tiers:
- Strong candidate: OOD success FA K=3 under 40%, OOD failure detection over
  85%, OOD Det@25 over 60%.
- Usable warning candidate: OOD success FA K=3 under 60%, OOD failure detection
  over 90%, OOD Det@25 over 70%.
- Hard-stop candidate: OOD success FA K=3 under 10% with failure detection over
  80%. This is unlikely in this campaign.

Primary old idea reused
-----------------------
TCN_history_k8, now retried on the clean target-object OOD split, with OOD rows
kept out of train/calib.

Primary new ideas added
-----------------------
1. Conditional RND with anonymous object-distance sets.
2. Denoising autoencoder normality scoring.
3. Seen-failure supervised risk models evaluated on unseen target objects.
4. Sequence models with action + history tokens.
5. No-object/no-proprio ablations to check whether context helps or just
   memorizes nuisance variation.
